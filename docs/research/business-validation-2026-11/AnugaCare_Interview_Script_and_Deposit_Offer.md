# Interview scripts and deposit offer page (drafts for your review)

> **Scope: dialysis scenario D1 only.** Prices, margins, scheduling limits and companion numbers in this file are for recurring dialysis visits and must not be applied to other service categories. Business-wide strategy is in `AnugaCare_Strategy_v2_Long_Term_Vision.md`; see `README_scope_and_index.md`.

Date: 2026-11-11. These are **drafts**. Nothing has been sent, published, built or posted, and no one has been contacted. The deposit page is copy and layout only (no code, no deployment). Using any of this needs your explicit approval and, for the page, a lawyer and CA check first.
Plans and prices come from `AnugaCare_Final_Pricing_and_Decision_Memo.md`. Companion pay and selection are in `AnugaCare_Companion_Model_Recruitment_and_Pay.md`.

**Ground rules for every conversation**
- Say who you are and why you are asking. This is research, not a sales call, and nobody is obliged to pay.
- Ask permission before taking notes. Do not record audio without consent.
- Collect the minimum. Do not ask for the patient's name, diagnosis details or medical records. "On dialysis" and sessions per week are enough.
- Never claim a companion is a nurse or gives medical care. Never promise a start date, a centre agreement or a price you cannot yet deliver.
- Do not give medical advice. If a patient seems unwell, suggest contacting their centre.
- Listen more than you talk. Ask for stories about the last two weeks, not opinions about the future.
- Do not mention the deposit until the end of the interview, and only to people who ask or who say yes to the price card.

## Part A. Family interview (30 to 35 minutes; 12 to 15 people)

**Who to ask:** adult children or spouses of dialysis patients aged 60+ in one area, introduced through people you already know or a centre you have visited. You choose the introductions.

### A1. Opening (2 min)
> "Thank you for talking to me. I am working on a service to help families get patients to and from dialysis. I am trying to understand how it works for you today. There is nothing to buy and no right answers. Is it all right if I take notes? I will not use your name or your parent's name."

### A2. Screening (3 min), record on the sheet
1. How many dialysis sessions a week? Has it changed in the last 3 months?
2. Which centre or hospital? Roughly how far from home?
3. What is the cost per session? (Ask: "roughly, so I understand what families spend.")
4. Who takes your parent today? How many hours does it take that person, door to door?

### A3. Today's reality (8 min), ask for the last two weeks
5. "Tell me about the last time your parent went to dialysis, from leaving home to coming back."
6. Who went with them? What did that person give up, for example work, leave, other duties?
7. What is hardest about it: the time, the travel, the waiting, finding someone, the journey home?
8. How does your parent feel after a session? Have there been difficult moments, for example dizziness, a fall, a delayed pickup?
9. Has a session ever been missed or late because no one could take them? What happened?
10. How do you travel today (own car, auto, cab, relatives)? About what does that cost per month?

### A4. What they have tried (4 min)
11. Have you hired an attendant, a driver or any service? What did it cost, and what went wrong or right?
12. If yes, would you do it again? Why or why not?
13. What would make you trust a stranger to take your parent to the centre and home?

### A5. The idea and the price (8 min)
Read the description once:
> "Imagine a trained, ID-checked companion, not a nurse and not medical, who collects your parent from home, takes them to the centre, hands them to the centre staff, goes away, and comes back at the end of the session to bring them home. You get a message at each step. If that companion is unwell, a replacement comes. You are not asked to sit there."

14. In your own words, what is that worth to you? (Wait. Do not suggest a number.)
15. **Price cards.** Show one card at a time and ask for a reaction before showing the next:
    - Card 1: ₹9,999 for 4 weeks, 12 round trips. Cab fare extra.
    - Card 2: ₹10,999 for 4 weeks, 12 round trips. Cab fare extra.
    - Card 3: ₹11,999 for 4 weeks, 12 round trips. Cab fare extra.
    - Card 4 (lighter): ₹7,799 for 12 trips home only (the companion meets your parent at the centre and brings them home). Cab fare extra.
    For each: "Would you pay this? Too cheap, fair, or too expensive?" Also ask: "At what price would you start to doubt the quality?" and "At what price would it be too much to consider?"
16. "If this existed next month, what would you do?"
17. "What would make you say no?"

### A6. Staying with the service (4 min)
18. "If the companion said, 'I know you now, pay me directly and I will charge less', what would you do and why?"
19. "What would make you stay with the service instead?"
20. "What guarantee would matter most: a backup companion within an hour, insurance for the trip, a record of who collected your parent, something else?"

### A7. Close (3 min)
21. "What did I not ask that I should have?"
22. "Is there anyone else, like a friend in a similar situation, who might be willing to talk?"
23. Only if they said yes to a price card: "We are deciding whether to launch in your area. If we do, would you like to hold a place with a refundable deposit? I can send the details. You are free to say no."
> "Thank you. If we go ahead, I will let you know. If you would rather I did not contact you, say so and I will delete your notes."

### A8. Interviewer notes sheet (one row per interview)
| Field | Fill with |
|---|---|
| ID (not name) | F01, F02… |
| Date, area, centre type | |
| Sessions per week | 2 / 3 / other |
| Session fee band | under ₹2,500 / ₹2,500 to 4,000 / ₹4,000+ |
| Who escorts, hours per trip, what they give up | |
| Post-session condition | steady / tired / unsteady |
| Past solutions and cost | |
| Reaction to each card | Too cheap / fair / too much, plus the reason |
| Would pay card 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 | Yes / maybe / no |
| Bypass answer, retention answer (verbatim) | |

| Fit score (section 2 scorecard of the final memo) | 0 to 7 |

**Reading the results:** count how many accept ₹917 per trip (card 2). Pass needs at least 5 of 12 at card 1 and at least 3 at card 2 or 3, with at least 8 describing a real recurring problem.

## Part B. Companion candidate interview (45 to 60 minutes) and pay test

**Who to ask:** candidates you choose (ex-hospital staff, GDA or HHA graduates, women re-entering work, two-wheeler riders, drivers). Do not advertise "join as a companion" until the pilot gates in the companion file are met.

### B1. Opening (3 min)
> "We are planning a service to take dialysis patients to the centre and home, with a handover to the centre staff. The role is a companion, not a nurse. You would not give medicines or medical care. I want to understand if it is a job you would want, and what would make it fair. Nothing is promised today."

### B2. Background (7 min)
1. Tell me about your recent work. Which hospitals or care jobs? How long?
2. Why did you leave your last job?
3. Do you hold a GDA, HHA, nursing or first-aid certificate?
4. Which languages do you speak with patients?
5. Which area do you live in, and which centres could you reach in 30 minutes?
6. Do you own a bike, auto, car, or none?

### B3. Availability (5 min)
7. Which days and hours could you reliably work? Morning, midday, evening?
8. Would you work as a top-up or as your main job?
9. Any family duties or other work (for example Rapido, delivery) that overlap?

### B4. Pay discovery (8 min), reservation pay first
10. "Before I tell you anything, what is the lowest amount you would take for one round trip: collecting a patient from home, taking them to the centre, handing over, then returning 4 to 5 hours later to bring them home? About 3.6 hours of active work, with a gap in between."
11. Then show three offers one at a time and ask for yes, maybe or no on each:
    - ₹330 per trip, plus ₹60 commute allowance (₹20 if you use your own two-wheeler).
    - ₹380 per trip, same allowance.
    - ₹430 per trip, same allowance.
12. "How would you use the gap between the two legs?"
13. "What would a minimum monthly guarantee need to be for you to commit to named shifts?"
14. "What would make this job better than your current one, and what would make it worse?"

### B5. Scenarios (15 min), listen for calm, boundaries and honesty
15. A patient looks dizzy at the centre door. What do you do?
16. The centre staff are late. The patient is tired and wants to go home alone. What do you do?
17. The family hands you ₹2,000 and says "buy medicine on the way". What do you do?
18. After two months the family says, "Come direct, we will pay you more." What do you do?
19. The patient refuses to leave the house on the day. What do you do?
20. You are late because of traffic. Who do you call and what do you say?
21. A patient falls getting out of the cab. What do you do?
22. The patient asks you to help them take a tablet. What do you do?

Marks: calm and sensible (0 to 2); respects scope (no medicine, no money) (0 to 2); honest about direct dealing (0 to 2); communicates clearly (0 to 2). Red flags: wants cash outside the platform, hostile to verification, gives clinical advice, brushes off safety.

### B6. Close (3 min)
23. "What questions do you have for me?"
24. "Are you willing to complete ID and background verification and two to three days of training, with paid shadow days?"
> "Thank you. I will let you know either way."

### B7. Candidate sheet
ID; pool; certificates; area; vehicle; availability; reservation pay; response to ₹330 / ₹380 / ₹430; guarantee asked; scenario score (0 to 8); red flags; decision (advance / hold / decline).

**Decision rule:** pick the **lowest offer where at least 4 candidates commit to named shifts**. If most say no at ₹430, or reservation pay is above about ₹550, stop and revisit the price or the employment route.

## Part C. Centre visit (10 to 15 minutes, with the manager or the in-charge nurse)

> "I am exploring a service that helps patients with transport and an accountable escort to your centre. It is non-clinical. I would like to understand how it should work with your team. I am not asking for any payment or referral."

1. How many sessions and shifts do you run? When are the busiest times?
2. How many patients come with a relative or attendant? Where do relatives wait?
3. Has crowding in the waiting area been a problem?
4. How often do patients arrive late, miss sessions or wait for pickup after a session? Why?
5. Would your staff release a patient to a named escort, authorised in writing by the family, with an OTP or ID check?
6. Would you accept a handover at reception, and may the escort leave during the session if the family can be reached by phone?
7. How long are patients observed before discharge, and who do you call if one is unwell?
8. What would you need from us (insurance proof, ID list, a protocol note)?
9. Is there anything that would make this a problem for you?
10. "Would you be willing to write a short note confirming the handover protocol you are comfortable with?"

Do not promise anything, do not claim clinical monitoring, and do not offer or accept payment for introductions.

## Part D. Deposit offer page (draft copy and layout)

**Status banner (top of page, always visible):** "Pre-launch. We have not started service yet. Reserving a place does not start a service. Your deposit is fully refundable."

Page variants (show **one** to each visitor, same page otherwise):
- Variant A: ₹9,999 per 4 weeks
- Variant B: ₹10,999 per 4 weeks
- Variant C: ₹11,999 per 4 weeks
- Variant D (lighter): ₹7,799 for 12 trips home only

### Section 1: Headline
**Get your 6 to 7 hours back on dialysis days.**
A trained, ID-checked companion takes your parent to the dialysis centre, hands them to the centre team, and brings them home. You are not asked to wait.

### Section 2: How it works (3 steps)
1. **We collect your parent from home** and travel with them to the centre.
2. **We hand them to the centre staff** and return later, after the session, to bring them home.
3. **You get a message at every step**: leaving home, handed over, collected, home safely.

### Section 3: What the plan includes (planned; do not publish anything not yet true)
- 12 round trips per 4-week plan (about three a week).
- A companion who is ID-verified and trained.
- A named companion and a second companion, so someone familiar always comes.
- A backup companion if yours is unwell (target within about an hour).
- A record of who collected your parent and when.
- Cab or auto fare is paid separately and prepaid at cost.

### Section 4: What it is not
Our companions are **not nurses and do not give medical care**. They do not handle medicines or money. The centre's staff look after the dialysis and medical needs.

### Section 5: Price (one variant per visitor)
**₹[9,999 / 10,999 / 11,999] for 4 weeks, 12 round trips.**
Fare extra (about ₹[2,900 to 4,700] a month depending on vehicle and distance).
Prices shown before GST. [CA to confirm whether GST applies and how to display it.]

### Section 6: Reserve your place
> "We are deciding where to start and are choosing the first families carefully. Reserve a place with a **refundable ₹500 deposit**."

How the deposit works:
- It holds a place in the first group in your area.
- **It is fully refundable** on request until you start service, and automatically if we have not started in your area within **60 days**. Refund within 7 days by the same UPI.
- When service starts, ₹500 is credited to your first plan.
- We will only start when a dialysis centre near you has agreed to the handover and companions are verified and trained. We will tell you before you pay anything more.

### Section 7: Form (minimum data)
- Your name and phone (for the deposit and updates).
- Your relationship to the patient.
- Area or pin code, and the dialysis centre name.
- Sessions per week (2 / 3 / more).
- Roughly the cost per session (under ₹2,500 / ₹2,500 to 4,000 / ₹4,000+).
- Usual session shift (morning / midday / evening).
- Who goes with your parent today (me / another relative / paid attendant / no one).
- Preferred plan (the one shown, plus "return trips only").
- Optional: any worry you want us to know.
- Consent checkbox (see below).
Do **not** ask for the patient's name, diagnosis, medical records or Aadhaar.

### Section 8: FAQ (honest answers)
- **Is this a nurse?** No. A trained companion. Not medical.
- **Why a deposit?** It shows families who genuinely want this, so we start in the right place. It is refundable.
- **When will you start?** Only once a centre near you agrees and companions are ready. We will tell you; if not within 60 days you are refunded.
- **What if the companion is unwell?** A backup companion, target within about an hour. [Publish only once true.]
- **Can I hire the companion directly later?** Yes, after six months through an introduction option. [Publish only once a lawyer approves.]
- **Is my information safe?** We collect the minimum, use it only for this reservation and your service, and delete it on request.
- **What if I change my mind?** Ask for the refund any time before the service starts.

### Section 9: Consent text (draft, lawyer to approve)
> "I agree that AnugaCare may contact me about this reservation. I understand the ₹500 deposit is refundable, that my information is used only for this purpose, and that I can ask for it to be deleted. I understand the companion is not a nurse and does not give medical care."

### Section 10: Footer
Business name and registered details, contact number, refund policy, privacy notice. [Fill once the entity and account exist.]

### Payment mechanics, to check before any money is taken `[I]`
- **Account:** UPI to a **business** account in the correct legal name. Do not use a personal account. If the entity is not ready, run the interview and "interest list" stages without money first.
- **CA:** treatment of a refundable deposit and any GST. **Lawyer:** refund terms, consent text, consumer-protection basics, the DPDP notice.
- Keep a ledger of each deposit and refund. Refund promptly. No shortcuts.

### Tracking sheet (one row per visitor or family)
Variant shown; date; area; centre; sessions per week; fee band; who goes today; deposit paid (Y/N, date); refund requested; objection; follow-up.

### How to read the result
| Result in 14 days (about 30 families shown the page) | Reading |
|---|---|
| 5 or more deposits, at least 2 on variant B or C | Price holds at ₹10,999 or above |
| 5 or more, all on variant A or D | Ceiling nearer ₹9,999 or Safe Return first |
| 2 to 4 | Weak: interview the refusals |
| 0 to 1 | Pause |

Report deposits per variant, and by session fee and frequency band. A deposit shows intent, not retention. The same friendly circle can say yes to a friend, so avoid selling only to your own network.
