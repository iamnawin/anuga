## 1. The answer in one page

**Position:** AnugaCare gives the family back the 7 to 8 hours they lose every dialysis day, and gives the patient a safe door-to-chair trip. It does not sit with the patient. It is not a nurse and does not give clinical care.

**Ideal customer:** a working adult child (or an out-of-city or overseas child) of a 60+ patient who dialyses three times a week at a private or corporate centre, lives within about 8 km, and is tired or unsteady after sessions. Not the patient who travels alone happily, not the free-scheme patient.

**Final pricing to test** (all `[E]`, fare extra, quoted ex-GST until the CA answers):

| Tier | Price | For | Contribution per trip |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Door-to-Door Care Plan** (core) | **₹10,999 per 4 weeks, 12 trips** (₹917 per trip). Extra trips ₹899. Pay-as-you-go ₹999 | Families who need the whole day back | **₹334** (36%) |
| **Safe Return** (feeder, for patients who travel alone) | **₹7,799 for 12 returns** (₹650 per return); test ₹6,499 as a floor | Patients who manage the session but are unsafe going home | ₹243 at ₹650; ₹134 at ₹549 |
| **Stay** (only when needed) | ₹1,499 to ₹1,799 per session | Frail patients, or centres that insist on an attendant | about ₹510 to ₹800 |
| **Shared Ride** (later, scale) | about ₹9,000+ per month all-in | ₹2,500-session families in a dense corridor | needs about 75 families in one corridor |
| **Fare** | Distance-band fixed price, auto option, prepaid wallet | All | pass-through |

**Test cells for the deposit pre-sale:** ₹9,999, ₹10,999 and ₹11,999 per 4-week plan, plus one Safe Return cell.
**Lowest price I would accept:** ₹9,999. Below it the model does not pay back. **If 18% GST applies and the price must be tax-inclusive, you need about ₹11,999 to ₹12,999.**

**Decision:** B. **Validate for about ₹60,000 to ₹80,000 first, then pilot.** Do not commit the ₹6 to ₹7 lakh until a centre accepts handover in writing and about 5 families pay a deposit.

## 2. What the pain really is, and who it is for

- **The patient mostly settles during the session.** You are right: once dialysis starts the patient rests, and many patients come alone, dialyse and leave. So the pain is **not** that the patient is lonely or unsupervised for 4 hours.
- **The pain is the family's:** one person gives up 7 to 8 hours (about 3 for travel and handover, 4 to 5 for the wait), 12 times a month. Indian studies of dialysis caregivers report mild to severe caregiver burden in a large share of caregivers (about 61% to 69% mild or worse in two studies) `[V]`, but they do not measure hours lost, so the time figure is `[E]`.
- **And the risky moment is the trip home.** After a session patients can be tired and light-headed. One study reports a median recovery time of about 4 hours and low blood pressure prolonging it `[V]` (abstract-level; clinical teams give individual advice). That is the reason a person should receive the patient at the door or the centre.
- **Therefore sell by need, not by diagnosis.** Dialysis is the entry because it is predictable, frequent and has a fixed schedule. The product is "door to chair, and back, with someone accountable".

### Who is not a fit (be strict)

| Not a fit | Why | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Patients who travel alone and are fine | No pain; you will not win on price | Offer Safe Return only for the trip home, and only if they ask |
| Free-scheme (white card) patients | Pay no session fee, unlikely to pay ₹10,999 `[V]` roughly 16,500 free-dialysis patients in Telangana | Later NGO or CSR channel, a separate idea |
| Twice-a-week patients | At 8 trips the plan never pays back | Qualify by frequency |
| Patients far from your micro-market | Travel eats the margin | Do not serve until density exists |

## 3. Is it worth it for the family? The ideal patient worked out

The cab or fuel is paid either way, so the **extra** cost of AnugaCare is the service fee, not the fare.

Time freed: 12 trips × about 7 hours = **84 hours a month**. Plan fee ₹10,999 means about **₹131 per hour freed**.

| Escorting family member's annual income | Value of 84 hours | Value ÷ fee |
|---|---|---|
| ₹3 L | ₹12,600 | 1.1× |
| ₹6 L | ₹25,200 | 2.3× |
| ₹12 L | ₹50,400 | 4.6× |
| ₹24 L | ₹100,800 | 9.2× |

It pays for itself if the family member's time is worth more than about ₹131 per hour (about ₹2.6 L a year). It only counts if the time is real: unpaid leave, lost work, or a child who lives elsewhere. Retired relatives with free time are poor targets.

**The ideal patient's monthly picture** (3 a week, 8 km, ₹4,000 per session, private centre):

| Item | ₹ per month |
|---|---|
| Dialysis fee (hospital's charge, not ours) | 48,000 |
| AnugaCare plan (12 trips) | 10,999 |
| Cab fare (8 km, cab mid) | about 4,700 |
| **Total escort cost** | **about 15,700 (33% on top of the dialysis fee)** |
| AnugaCare contribution (12 × ₹334) | about 4,000 |

At ₹2,500 per session the same escort is about 52% on top, a hard sell. At 13 trips a month the bill is about ₹16,900 and contribution about ₹4,350.

## 4. How to convince customers

**Message, in order:** (1) "Get your 7 hours back, 12 days a month." (2) "A trained, ID-verified person brings your parent to the centre and home, with an OTP handover and a message to you." (3) "If she is ill, a replacement arrives within about an hour." (4) "One monthly plan, one wallet for the fare." (5) "Not a nurse; no medical care."

| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| "I will sit there myself." | "Of course. This is for the days you cannot, or the 12 days a month that cost you a working day. Try it for two weeks." |
| "I will hire the companion directly." | "You can after six months; here is how. Until then you get a backup, cover and records. A freelancer cannot guarantee that if she is unwell." |
| "My father goes alone." | "Then keep that. Safe Return covers only the trip home, on days he is weak." |
| "₹10,999 is too much." | "Compare with a ₹1,000-a-day attendant `[V]`, plus your lost day. We start with a two-week trial. If not worth it, stop." |
| "Is she a nurse?" | "No. She does not handle medicines or clinical care. The centre's staff do. She brings him to them and home." |
| "Will the centre allow it?" | "We have asked the centre for written confirmation. Here is the protocol." (Only say this once true.) |

Proof the customer can see: ID and photo, OTP at pickup, a message when the patient is handed over, a weekly report, a written backup promise, and referrals from other families.

## 5. How to convince centres and hospitals

**What is in it for a centre** (`[E]`, verify in the visits; I found no centre statement either way):

1. **Fewer missed and late sessions.** Missed treatments are linked to more hospitalisation. A recent retrospective study reports about 2× the 7-day hospitalisation rate after a missed treatment, and transport problems are a recurring cause of misses (11% of missed treatments in one survey; 39% of a surveyed group said they had missed a session due to a transport provider's mistake) `[V]`, abstract-level and mostly US data. A reliable ride helps adherence.
2. **Smoother chair turnover.** On-time arrival and on-time pickup help the centre run each shift. Fewer patients waiting for rides after the session.
3. **Less crowding.** Fewer relatives waiting in the area frees space and reduces staff time managing visitors.
4. **A named, verified person for discharge.** Staff know who is collecting the patient, with the family's authorisation and an OTP, rather than chasing relatives by phone.
5. **No cost, no clinical overlap, no referral fee.** The centre keeps clinical control. Never pay a centre for introductions.

**The ask (written, one page):** (a) accept a handover at reception from a registered AnugaCare companion with ID and the family's authorisation; (b) a named contact; (c) agree who calls the family if the patient is unwell at discharge; (d) share shift timings; (e) permit the companion to leave when a responsible person is reachable by phone `[I]`.

**Do not claim:** clinical monitoring, infection-control exceptions, or that you replace staff. **Do not promise** anything until the centre's answers are written down.

**If the centre says no to leaving:** the model becomes Stay (₹1,499 to ₹1,799 per session) or fails for that centre. Choose centres that say yes.

## 7. Beyond dialysis: later, in order

Hypotheses only; visit patterns are general knowledge, not sourced. Companion cost uses ₹110 per loaded hour for waiting designs.

| Service | Visits per month | Contribution per family-month `[E]` | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dialysis (3 a week) | 12 | about ₹3,700 to ₹4,000 | Core: frequent, predictable, long tenure |
| Chemotherapy day care | about 2.5 | about ₹800 | Same drop-and-return fit; low frequency |
| Radiotherapy course | about 22 | about ₹5,900 | High volume, only 1 to 2 months |
| Physiotherapy / cardiac rehab | about 10 | about ₹2,000 | Moderate, short tenure |
| Elderly OPD and follow-ups | about 1.5 | about ₹800 | High effort, low frequency |

(Simplified cost method; the engine gives ₹334 per dialysis trip against ₹311 here.)
**Sequence:** prove dialysis in one micro-market (about 30 paying families, on-time ≥95%, retention steady), then add chemo day care and follow-ups for the same families and centres, then radiotherapy and physiotherapy as a volume tier. Do not run them in parallel. Short-tenure services raise acquisition cost per rupee earned.

## 8. How this succeeds: the conditions

| # | Must be true | Evidence needed | Fails if |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | At least 2 centres accept handover in writing | Written note from each | None do |
| 2 | Families accept about ₹917 per trip | At least 5 refundable deposits, at least 2 at ₹10,999 or higher | Under 3 of 12 interviewees accept ₹917 |
| 3 | Companions can be hired at ≤₹430 effective per trip | 4 committed candidates in named shifts | They ask over about ₹550 or would go direct below it |
| 4 | Frequency near 12 per month | Interviews: sessions per week | Most are twice a week |
| 5 | Density in one micro-market | 2 to 3 centres within about 8 km, families clustered | Families scattered |
| 6 | GST works | CA opinion | 18% inclusive and price cannot rise |
| 7 | Bypass is under about 10% | Retention by pair, weekly early-warning | Above 25% |
| 8 | Overhead stays lean (about ₹82k to ₹112k) until about 30 families | Monthly cost review | Hires ahead of volume |

Numbers behind this (S1, 12 trips, ₹10,999, v1 overhead curve and ramp): break-even about **28 families** for ₹112k overhead (21 for ₹82k, 36 for ₹142k); **month 10** and cash +₹27 L at month 24 for ₹4,000+ families; **month 14** and +₹1.4 L for ₹2,500 families (half the ramp); with 10% bypass month 11; at 8 trips per family never. At ₹799 the same plan never breaks even (cash −₹36 L) and with absorbed GST at ₹10,999 it never breaks even either. All `[E]`, and the ramp and overhead are assumptions.

## 12. Limits

- Every price and cost is an estimate. The ramp and the v1 overhead curve are assumptions. Willingness to pay, centre handover, companion supply, GST and bypass rates are unknown until interviews, centres, a CA and a lawyer say otherwise.
- Hospital benefits are my reasoning. Clinical studies cited are abstract-level, mostly US, and show association, not that AnugaCare will change outcomes. Do not use them as medical claims.
- Caregiver-burden studies do not measure hours lost; the 7-hour figure is mine.
- Visit patterns beyond dialysis are general knowledge, not sourced.
- Sources this round: [missed sessions and transport (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7380335), [why patients miss dialysis](https://www.hcplive.com/view/why-patients-miss-dialysis-treatments-with-steven-brunelli-md), [caregiver burden, Sikkim](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35496347/), [NIDDK post-dialysis fatigue](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/-/media/Files/News/Meetings/NIDDK-2023-Post-Dialysis-Fatigue-Executive-Summary_508.pdf), plus the sources in earlier files.
- Nobody was contacted and nothing was spent.
