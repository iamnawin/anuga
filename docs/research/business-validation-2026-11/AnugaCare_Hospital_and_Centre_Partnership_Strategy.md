# Hospital and dialysis-centre partnership strategy

Date: 2026-11-11. Part of strategy v2.0 (`AnugaCare_Strategy_v2_Long_Term_Vision.md`). Draft for founder review.
Labels: `[V]` found in a source, `[E]` my estimate or reasoning, `[U]` not found, `[I]` needs a conversation or a document to settle.
Nothing here has been sent or discussed with any centre. No centre has given permission, endorsement or any commitment. This document assumes none.

---

## 1. Starting position

- **I found no published policy at any Hyderabad centre on external, non-clinical companions handing patients over or collecting them.** That is an absence of evidence. Every centre is **unknown** until it tells you in writing.
- Nothing found supports an endorsement, a preferred-provider status or any approval of AnugaCare. The only government guidance I located mentions attendants and caregivers wearing masks in a dialysis unit during COVID `[V]` and says nothing about handover. The NHSRC guideline for the government programme could not be read in this session `[U]`.
- Centres run clinical operations under their own rules. What AnugaCare offers is **operational reliability around the clinical session**: an accountable person arriving on time, a verified handover, and a pickup that does not leave a patient waiting.

**Strategy in one line:** earn a **written, narrow, revocable handover protocol** by being useful and reliable, never by claiming endorsement.

---

## 2. Principles

1. **No assumed permission.** Ask, listen, get it in writing. Do not start until a centre says yes to specifics.
2. **No claimed endorsement.** Never state or imply that a centre recommends, approves or partners with AnugaCare unless it has said so in writing and approved the wording.
3. **Patient and staff first.** The centre's clinical judgement and infection control always win. A companion never overrides staff.
4. **Verified handovers.** Every handover has identity, authorisation and a timestamp.
5. **Reliability is the product.** Measure it and show it.
6. **No money for access.** No fees, referral payments, gifts, or exclusivity.
7. **Easy to stop.** Either side can end the arrangement at any time.
8. **Minimal data.** Share only what a handover needs.
9. **Fair to competitors** (centre-run transport, other vendors): do not disparage.

| **Group management** (for chains) | Brand, legal, standardisation | A written SOP, a sponsor, a metrics summary |

## 4. Engagement ladder and permission map

Each centre sits at one level. The level controls what AnugaCare may do and say.

| Level | Status | What you may do | What you may say publicly |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S0 Unknown** | No contact | Research only | Nothing about the centre |
| **S1 Asked** | Meeting held, no answer yet | Collect answers to the eight questions | Nothing about the centre |
| **S2 Informal "no objection"** | Verbal comfort only | Plan, do shadow runs outside the unit, not with patients | Nothing about the centre |
| **S3 Written protocol** | Short signed or emailed handover protocol for a limited pilot | Run the pilot with 3 to 5 consenting families | "Our companions follow the handover protocol agreed with [centre]" only if wording is approved in writing |
| **S4 Operating** | Stable operation for 8+ weeks, review held | Scale within agreed limits | Same, wording approved |
| **S5 Declined or revoked** | Said no, or withdrew | Stop; do not argue or work around | Nothing; withdraw any mention |

**Language rules:** use "serves patients who attend [centre]" only at S3 or above and only if the centre approves the wording. Never use a centre's name or logo for marketing without written permission. Never say "approved", "endorsed", "recommended" or "official partner" unless they use those words in writing.

## 5. The ask and the give

### What AnugaCare asks (one page, in writing)
1. Accept a **handover at reception** from a **registered companion** carrying ID, with the family's written authorisation and a code or OTP.
2. A **named contact** on the centre side.
3. Agreement on **who calls the family** if the patient is unwell at discharge.
4. **Shift timings**, and where possible a **heads-up about 30 minutes before discharge**.
5. Permission for the companion to **leave** while the session runs, provided a responsible person is reachable by phone `[I]`.
6. A **stop button**: either side may end the pilot without reason.

### What AnugaCare gives
1. **On-time arrival** and pickup, with monthly numbers.
2. A **registry of verified companions** with photos and IDs, updated when anyone joins or leaves.
3. An **incident log** shared promptly, and a named person who answers.
4. **Insurance and vetting proof** (once held).
5. **Training offered**: the centre may review the companion module and add its own do's and don'ts.
6. **Less crowding and fewer chasing calls** (to be measured, not promised).
7. No cost to the centre.

### What we never ask or offer
Endorsement, logo use without permission, patient lists, exclusivity, preferential treatment, referral fees, gifts, access to clinical areas, access to medical records, or any clinical task for companions.

## 6. Draft verified handover protocol (SOP) for review `[E]`

**Before the first trip**
1. Family completes consent and written authorisation naming the companions allowed to bring and collect the patient.
2. Centre receives the registry entry for those companions (name, photo, ID reference) and a contact number.

**Drop**
3. Companion arrives at least 10 minutes before the session start and reports to reception with ID.
4. Companion shows the OTP or code sent to the family and centre contact; staff confirm identity.
5. Staff accept the patient by name; handover time is recorded by the companion's app or log and the centre's own record.
6. The companion leaves only after staff confirm acceptance, and stays reachable by phone.

**Pickup**
7. Staff (or the companion, on a heads-up message) confirm the patient is ready to leave. The companion arrives and shows ID.
8. Staff confirm the companion against the registry and the family's authorisation, and release the patient only if the patient is cleared to leave by their own assessment.
9. The companion sends the family a message at departure and on arrival home; the family confirms receipt.

**Exceptions**
10. If the patient appears unwell at any point, staff decide on care; the companion does not transport the patient and calls the family and the centre contact.
11. If the companion is late beyond 15 minutes the centre is told at once; the centre may proceed per its own policy.
12. If the registered companion cannot come, AnugaCare tells the centre and the family before the pickup; a different person is released the patient only with a fresh authorisation.
13. Any incident is logged the same day and shared with the centre contact within 24 hours.

**Records:** time of arrival, handover, release; names; messages sent; incident notes. Kept under the privacy rules in the consent. No clinical information is recorded by companions.

## 7. Operational reliability metrics shared with centres

| Metric | Target to aim for `[E]` | Action if missed |
|---|---|---|
| On-time drop (arrival before session start) | 95%+ | Review within 48 hours, share root cause |
| Pickup wait after patient is ready | Median under 15 min; no wait over 30 min | Adjust dispatch and heads-up arrangement |
| Handover completeness (ID, authorisation, timestamp) | 100% | Retrain and audit |
| Incidents (any, serious) | Zero serious; all closed | Suspend the companion; report to the centre |
| Late or missed trips | Under 3% | Plan a backup; tell the centre |
| Complaints | Zero unresolved over 7 days | Escalate |

Monthly one-page report. **Suspension triggers:** one serious incident, two protocol breaches, or on-time below 90% for four weeks pauses new trips at that centre until resolved.

## 8. Different institution types

| Type | Approach |
|---|---|
| **Corporate hospital-run dialysis units** | Decisions may sit with administration and legal; slower; ask for a short SOP and insurance proof; expect questions on liability and security. Strong brand risk, so keep wording tight |
| **Standalone and chain dialysis centres** | Faster decisions; manager and medical lead; pilot with a few patients; chains need group sign-off for any wider use |
| **Government and PM National Dialysis Programme centres** | Not a first target: programme rules, procurement and free-service context. Read the NHSRC guideline before any approach `[I]` |
| **Nursing homes and clinics** (chemotherapy, physiotherapy, radiotherapy) | Later phases; the same ladder |
| **Senior living communities** | A similar protocol for resident visits and outings; the community manager is the decision maker |

## 9. Expected objections and honest answers

| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| "We don't allow outsiders." | "Understood. Is there a narrow version you could accept, such as handover at reception only? We will follow whatever you set." |
| "Who is liable if something happens?" | "The companion's role is non-clinical and limited to transit and handover. Here is our insurance and incident process. We will accept your conditions." (Do not claim insurance you do not hold.) |
| "Our staff cannot take on more." | "The protocol is built to reduce phone chasing. We will adapt to your workflow and stop if it adds work." |
| "We run our own transport." | "We are not trying to replace it. We cover patients who need a person with them, and we can step back." |
| "Will you refer patients to us or pay us?" | "No. We do not pay for introductions." |
| "Are you medically qualified?" | "No. The companions are non-clinical. Staff handle all medical care." |
| "Can we recommend you?" | "Thank you. We would only use it if you approve the wording in writing." |

**Walk-away signs:** a centre asks for payment, wants exclusivity, wants patient lists, or wants AnugaCare to take on clinical tasks.

## 11. One-page leave-behind (draft text)

**AnugaCare: verified handovers for patients who need an escort**
AnugaCare provides trained, ID-checked, non-clinical companions to bring patients to and from scheduled visits. We do not provide medical care and do not replace your staff.

*What we propose:* a written handover protocol for a four-week pilot with a few consenting patients. Registered companions arrive on time with ID and the family's authorisation; your staff confirm identity and accept or release the patient; every handover is timestamped; incidents are shared within 24 hours.

*What we ask:* a named contact, your shift times, and your rules. Either side can stop at any time.

*What you get:* a monthly reliability report, a registry of verified companions, and no cost to you. We do not pay referral fees, ask for endorsement, or use your name without written permission.

*Contact:* [name, phone]. [Insurance and vetting details once in place.]
